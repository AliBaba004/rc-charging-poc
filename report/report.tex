\begin{abstract}
This document introduces the charging behaviour of a resistor-capacitor (RC) network and derives the capacitor voltage at one time constant.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

When a capacitor $C$ is charged through a series resistor $R$ from a constant supply voltage $V_0$, the capacitor voltage rises according to

\[
V(t) = V_0\left(1-e^{-t/RC}\right).
\]

The product $RC$ is called the time constant,

\[
\tau = RC.
\]

\section{Voltage at the Time Constant}

Substituting $t=\tau$ into the charging equation,

\[
V(\tau)=V_0\left(1-e^{-\tau/RC}\right)
\]

Since $\tau=RC$,

\[
V(\tau)=V_0\left(1-e^{-1}\right)
\]

\[
V(\tau)\approx V_0(1-0.368)
\]

\[
V(\tau)\approx 0.632V_0
\]

For $V_0=5.0$ V,

\[
V(\tau)\approx 0.632 \times 5.0 \approx 3.16\text{ V}
\]
